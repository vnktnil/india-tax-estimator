1.144ADA eligibility: gross receipts up to Rs.50L, or up to Rs.75L if cash receipts are 5% or less of total receipts (i.e. 95%+ received digitally/through banking channels). Exceeding this — or receiving more than 5% in cash — means 44ADA cannot be used. [Sec 44ADA(1), proviso]

1.1Rental income: 30% standard deduction applied automatically. [Sec 24(a)]

1.1Other income (interest, dividends etc) added to slab income. [Sec 56]
7
1.1Home loan interest (Section 24b) — the key things to know:
• Self-occupied (you live there): interest deduction capped at Rs.2,00,000/year, combined across up to 2 self-occupied properties. Old Regime only — New Regime gives no deduction here.
• Let-out (rented to someone else): interest is fully deductible with no cap, netted against that property's rental income. This works under both regimes since it's part of computing the rental income itself, not a separate deduction.
• Vacant, never rented (empty all year, not your home): treated the same as self-occupied — annual value Nil — as long as it's within your first 2 such properties. It is NOT automatically taxed just for sitting empty; the "vacancy relief" some people expect only applies to a property that WAS let during the year (Sec 23(1)(c)), not one that was never let at all.
• Let for part of the year, vacant the rest (e.g. tenant left mid-year, new one hasn't moved in yet): enter only the actual rent you received for the months it was let, NOT a full-year estimate — the part-year projector above is built for exactly this. Per Sec 23(1)(c), your actual (lower) rent received is used as the annual value instead of the full expected rent, precisely because the shortfall was caused by the vacancy.
• 3rd+ self-occupied or vacant property: automatically treated as "deemed let-out" and taxed on notional rent even though not actually rented, per Sec 23(1)(a). You'll need to estimate a fair rental value for it.
• Pre-construction interest (paid before the house was ready to move into): can't be claimed immediately — it's banked and released in 5 equal yearly instalments starting the year construction finishes.
• If a let-out property's net position is a loss, only Rs.2,00,000 of that loss can offset your other income this year; any excess carries forward for up to 8 years (not tracked by this tool).
This is a newer, more complex area of the calculator — the ⚠ caveats shown alongside your property entries flag the specific points that should be confirmed with a CA before relying on the numbers. [Sec 24(b), Sec 23]
8
1.1HRA (Sec 10(13A)) and Section 80GG — rent you pay for your own home:
• If you receive HRA (part of salary): exemption is the least of (a) HRA actually received, (b) 50% of Basic+DA in a metro city or 40% elsewhere, (c) rent paid minus 10% of Basic+DA. "Basic+DA" is NOT your gross/CTC salary — check your payslip. For FY 2025-26, only Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, and Chennai count as metro (Bengaluru/Pune/Hyderabad/Ahmedabad join from FY 2026-27, not this year).
• If you don't receive HRA (e.g. most 44ADA/self-employed income): Section 80GG allows a deduction of the least of (a) Rs.60,000/year, (b) 25% of total income, (c) rent paid minus 10% of total income — but ONLY if neither you, your spouse, nor your minor child owns a residential property in the city you live/work in.
• Both are Old Regime only. Owning a rented-out property elsewhere while you personally rent your own home doesn't block either claim — the two are independent, as long as the "no property in your own city" condition is met for 80GG. [Sec 10(13A), Rule 2A, Sec 80GG]

1.2Capital gains only arise when you actually sell, redeem, or otherwise transfer an asset — not simply from its value going up while you hold it. An investment worth more than you paid, but not yet sold, has no tax impact this year. Grouped by tax rate once a gain is realised:
• Equity/MF STCG — flat 20% [Sec 111A]
• Equity/MF LTCG — 12.5% above Rs.1.25L exempt [Sec 112A]
• Slab-Rate Gains (debt MF, short-term property/gold/unlisted) — taxed at your slab rate [Sec 50AA for debt MF]
• Gold/Unlisted LTCG — flat 12.5%, no exemption [Sec 112]
• Property LTCG — compares 12.5% flat vs 20% with indexation (pre-23-Jul-2024 purchases only), uses whichever is lower [Finance (No. 2) Act 2024, proviso to Sec 112]
"Transfer" is defined broadly (sale, exchange, redemption, maturity, and a few other specific events) — but simply holding an asset that's appreciated is never itself a taxable event. [Sec 2(47)]
10
2.180C covers a combined Rs.1,50,000 limit across several things, not any one investment alone:
• EPF (Employees' Provident Fund) — only if you're salaried and your employer runs a PF scheme. It's deducted automatically from your salary every month (check your payslip or Form 16) — you don't do anything to set it up or contribute manually. Only your own share counts here, not your employer's matching contribution.
• PPF (Public Provident Fund) — a completely separate, optional savings account anyone can open at a bank or post office (whether salaried, self-employed, or 44ADA). You choose how much to deposit yourself, up to Rs.1,50,000/year on its own. Most people don't have both EPF and PPF — enter whichever applies to you, or both if you genuinely have both.
• ELSS — equity mutual funds with a 3-year lock-in
• Life insurance premiums — for self, spouse, or children
• Home loan principal repayment (not interest — that's separate, under Sec 24b, not currently in this calculator)
• Children's tuition fees, NSC, 5-year tax-saving FDs
Add up everything you already have (EPF + insurance + home loan principal often fills much of this before you invest anything new) and enter the total, capped at Rs.1,50,000. This does NOT include your own NPS contribution under Sec 80CCD(1) if you're claiming the extra Rs.50,000 under 80CCD(1B) below instead — see that note for how the two interact. [Sec 80C]
11
2.180D covers health insurance premiums (not general medical bills, except for uninsured senior citizens):
• Self/Spouse/Children bucket — premiums for a policy covering yourself, spouse, and dependent children. Rs.25,000 normally, Rs.50,000 if you or anyone covered is 60+.
• Parents bucket — a completely separate limit for premiums paid for either parent's health insurance, whether or not they're financially dependent on you. Rs.25,000 normally, Rs.50,000 if either parent is 60+.
These two buckets are independent — maximum combined claim (both buckets, both senior citizens) is Rs.1,00,000. Premiums must be paid via bank transfer/card/UPI/cheque, not cash (preventive health check-ups up to Rs.5,000, included within these same limits, are the one exception that can be paid in cash). [Sec 80D]
12
2.1NPS (National Pension System) and savings account interest — three separate things, easy to mix up:
• 80CCD(1) — your own regular NPS contribution: sits INSIDE the Rs.1,50,000 80C limit above, not separate. If you contribute to NPS yourself, include it in the 80C total, don't enter it again here.
• 80CCD(1B) — an EXTRA Rs.50,000 for your own NPS contribution, genuinely separate from the 80C cap. This is the one that lets you push your own-contribution deduction up to Rs.2,00,000 total (Rs.1.5L via 80C + Rs.50K here). Old Regime only.
• 80CCD(2) — your employer's NPS contribution (not your own money): capped at 14% of Basic+DA. This is the ONE deduction in this whole group that works under BOTH regimes — it's often the single biggest tax-saving lever left once you've moved to New Regime.
• 80TTA — savings account interest: up to Rs.10,000 of interest earned on a regular savings account (not fixed deposits or recurring deposits). Old Regime only. If you're 60+, a higher Rs.50,000 version (80TTB) applies instead, covering FDs too — not currently modelled separately here.
"Basic+DA" for the 80CCD(2) cap is the same figure entered for HRA above — you don't need to enter it twice. [Sec 80CCD, Sec 80TTA]

3.1Switching regimes is NOT free-form if you have 44ADA/business income. Unlike salary-only taxpayers (who can pick a regime every year directly in ITR-1/2), anyone filing ITR-3 or ITR-4 must file Form 10-IEA to opt for the Old Regime, and gets only one lifetime switch back to New Regime after opting out. Choosing Old Regime this year is not risk-free to reverse later — confirm with your CA before filing Form 10-IEA. [Form 10-IEA, Sec 115BAC(6)]
